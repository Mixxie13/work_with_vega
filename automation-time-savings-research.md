# Automation time-savings research

Reviewed September 30, 2026.

## Figure used in the portfolio

**3.6 hours per worker per week**: the average savings reported by people using automation at work in Slack’s **2023 State of Work** survey. This is a published, self-reported research benchmark. It does not measure Vega Morada’s project results or promise the same savings for every client.

The portfolio displays “3.6 hrs” and “Average saved / worker / week”. At the owner's request, the visible research note and source link were removed; this file retains the provenance for maintenance.

## Primary source and methodology

[Salesforce: What’s Holding Employees Back from Being More Productive? New Slack Research Holds Answers](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-work-stats-2023/?bc=OTH), published May 4, 2023.

Slack commissioned the research with Qualtrics. The full survey covered 18,149 desk workers and executives across nine countries, conducted February 24–March 21, 2023. The hours figure concerns respondents who used automation; the article does not give the size of that subgroup. Savings were respondents’ estimates, rather than independently timed before-and-after results. The Philippines was not included in the country sample.

The report is from 2023, not a new 2026 measurement. A later [Slack workflow automation article](https://slack.com/blog/productivity/a-guide-to-business-workflow-automation), published November 3, 2025, repeats the same finding and identifies the original study.

## Why this figure

It is an explicitly published average across automation users, with a clear unit and disclosed methodology. It covers automation broadly rather than only a specific AI assistant or job role. It replaces the previous assumption of one hour saved per day.

This external benchmark cannot establish personal portfolio impact. A measured client figure would require the manual task time, remaining handling and review time after automation, and actual task volume over a defined period.
